The four elasticities on one card

Every elasticity is the same fraction: the percentage change in quantity on top, the percentage change in whatever caused it underneath. Only the denominator changes.

MeasureFormulaWhat the sign tells youWhat the size tells you
Price elasticity of demand %ΔQd ÷ %ΔP Negative, by the law of demand. Usually quoted as an absolute value. Above 1, elastic and a price cut raises revenue. Below 1, inelastic and a price rise raises revenue.
Price elasticity of supply %ΔQs ÷ %ΔP Positive. Sellers offer more when price rises. Above 1, output is easy to expand. Below 1, capacity is stuck, which matters most in the short run.
Income elasticity %ΔQd ÷ %ΔIncome Positive for a normal good, negative for an inferior one. Above 1, a luxury whose sales swing harder than the economy. Between 0 and 1, a necessity that holds up in a downturn.
Cross-price elasticity %ΔQd of A ÷ %ΔP of B Positive means substitutes, negative means complements. How close the relationship is. Competition authorities use exactly this to decide who your rivals really are.

What makes demand elastic

  • Close substitutes existOne brand of vanilla is elastic. Dessert as a whole is not.
  • The good is a big share of the budgetNobody shops around over a stick of chewing gum.
  • It is a want, not a needNecessities and habits absorb price rises.
  • Time to adjustAlmost everything is more elastic in the long run, which is why a profitable price rise this quarter can bleed share for three years.
  • How narrowly you define the marketElasticity is not a property of a product. It is a property of a product, a market definition and a time horizon.

What makes supply elastic

  • Spare capacityAn idle production line can answer a price rise this week.
  • Storable outputFrozen stock can be released; fresh cream cannot wait.
  • Mobile inputsIf labour and equipment can be hired in quickly, supply stretches.
  • Time to adjustSupply is nearly fixed on the day, flexible over a season, and very flexible over years.
  • Natural limitsLand and prime retail pitches are close to perfectly inelastic, which is why their owners absorb taxes on them.

Three results worth taking into a meeting

Slope is not elasticity

A single straight demand curve runs from perfectly elastic at the top to perfectly inelastic at the bottom. Anyone who describes a product as simply "elastic" has skipped the question of at which price.

Revenue peaks where elasticity equals one

Above that price you are in the elastic half and should cut. Below it you are in the inelastic half and are leaving money on the table. Note this maximises revenue, not profit, which also needs marginal cost.

Who writes the cheque is not who pays

Legal incidence is set by the statute; economic incidence is set by elasticity. Tax the side that cannot escape, and that side carries it whichever name is on the return.
