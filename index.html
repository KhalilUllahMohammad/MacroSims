Pakistan’s economic story

From Mohenjo-daro to Gwadar

Twelve moments from 4,500 years of trade, money, water and roads in what is now Pakistan. Each one links to a simulator where you can see the economics at work.

Indus civilisationColonial port cityIndependent PakistanToday
c. 2600–1900 BCE

Mohenjo-daro, a planned city

Grid streets, covered drains and brick houses on the Indus. Its craftsmen specialised and its merchants traded as far as Mesopotamia.

c. 2600–1900 BCE

A shared standard of weight

Cube-shaped stone weights were standardised across the whole Indus region. You cannot measure prices or output without a common unit.

Connect it: Measuring the price levelCPI in Five Steps→
1869–1874

Karachi builds its harbour

A giant “Titan” crane set the blocks of the Manora breakwater. By 1899 Karachi was South Asia’s largest exporter of wheat and cotton.

Connect it: Exports in the current accountBoP Quest: Pakistan→
1890

A trading city

A crowded street in colonial Karachi. Every stall and cart is a small market where supply meets demand and a price is struck.

Connect it: How prices clear a marketDemand & Supply→
1920s

The Khyber Pass Railway

Built through the pass to the Afghan border, the line climbs by zig-zags and tunnels. The photo shows a steam special in 1993.

Connect it: Trade and the open economyMundell-Fleming Game→
1948

Pakistan gets a central bank

The State Bank of Pakistan began work in 1948 in Karachi’s former Imperial Bank of India building, which is now the SBP Museum.

Connect it: Monetary policy and interest ratesIS-LM Model→
1967

Mangla Dam

Built on the Jhelum after the 1960 Indus Waters Treaty to store water for irrigation and to generate hydroelectric power.

Connect it: More capacity moves LRAS rightAS-AD Model→
Late 1960s

The Green Revolution

High-yielding wheat varieties, fertiliser and tubewells lifted Punjab’s harvests. Bigger crops raise a question: what happens to farm prices?

Connect it: Bumper harvests and inelastic demandElasticity Simulator→
1976

Tarbela Dam

One of the world’s largest earth-filled dams, on the Indus. More water and power raise what the economy can produce in the long run.

Connect it: Natural output rises: both curves shiftDAD-DAS Model→
1997

Motorway M-2

The 375 km Lahore–Islamabad motorway cuts through the Salt Range. Big public projects like this draw on the nation’s saving.

Connect it: Public investment and crowding outLoanable Funds Lab→
2007

Gwadar deep-sea port

A new port on the Arabian Sea, later a centrepiece of the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor. Foreign investment flows show up in the balance of payments.

Connect it: FDI in the financial accountBoP Quest: Pakistan→
